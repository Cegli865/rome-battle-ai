# Strategy for the AI general

Tactical advice: sound for most situations, but the general may depart from it when the situation clearly
calls for something else. The facts it builds on are in [rules.md](rules.md). Re-read before every battle
(no server restart needed); only lines starting with "- " are sent to the model.

- Do not risk your general: keep him close behind your fighting units - about 40-80 m behind the line, near enough for his RALLY to reach them, and in a town follow the army in through the same way - but never in the melee himself. Commit him to fight only against routing enemies, or as a last resort when the battle is otherwise lost.
- When the morale of your units near the general drops (units wavering, routing or taking heavy losses), switch the general's RALLY ability on (rally=true); switch it off again once they have steadied.
- Do not push artillery in front of your own infantry line: keep it at least ~40 m behind the frontmost infantry, and do not march it towards targets more than ~400 m away. Enemy cavalry will destroy exposed artillery. Artillery fires from the rear, covered by infantry.
- Missile units (archers, slingers, skirmishers) must not get caught in melee: when enemy infantry or cavalry come within ~50 m, pull them back ~70 m behind your line. When a missile unit is out of ammunition, withdraw it behind the army (or use it as a reserve).
- In settlement battles: if you have units capable of a phalanx (hoplites, phalangites, pikemen), use them in the streets (they stand in phalanx) - block the streets the enemy uses on his way to the town square with them, one unit across a street - so their front is narrow and they cannot be outflanked.
- Artillery target priority - pick the highest one that is within range: 1) town defences when you attack a town: wall sections, towers, gates (attack_building; never where your own siege equipment is); 2) enemy artillery; 3) big clusters of enemy units; 4) the enemy general; 5) any other enemy unit. Do not move your artillery in front of your infantry line to reach a target, and never shoot over your own siege tower, ladders or ram (a WARNING in the plan says so).
- Cavalry against spearmen: do not charge spear units or units in phalanx or schiltrom from the front. Hit them in the flank or the rear (attack_from=left/right/rear) or pick another target. A frontal charge on spears is only a last resort.
- When attacking a town, never leave units standing idle near the walls, in reach of the wall towers. Only move up to the walls to break through them, with siege towers, ladders, a battering ram at the gate, or through a breach in the wall. Until then keep units out of tower range.
- In a siege battle every move (holding walls, fighting in the streets, breaching) only serves the town square.
- When defending a town, always keep at least one solid unit on the town square. The moment any enemy unit comes within ~60 m of the square, counter-attack it with the 2-3 nearest units at a run.
- When the game's AI uses your siege equipment, plan the rest of the army around it: follow up through the gates and wall sections it opens, and put those units into your attacking groups once they are yours.
- When attacking a town, entering through one way in is fine (e.g. the first open breach, without waiting for siege equipment). Inside, when more than one street leads onto the square, attack the square from at least two sides at the same time: split into groups that reach it along different streets (see ROUTES TO THE SQUARE; ask for more with route_requests), use the march times so they arrive together, and hit the defenders in front and in the flank or rear. Defenders held from one side and struck from another break much faster. Go in with one concentrated push only when the defenders are so few that it is enough on its own, or when there really is only one way onto the square.
- Druids: keep them just behind your fighting line, close to the melee but not in it, and have them chant (use_ability) while your units nearby are fighting. They chant only while they stand still: give them their own spot (target_point) 20-40 m behind the fight and let them stand there - do not march them along with the army into the melee. The same goes for screeching women and their curse, which lowers the morale of nearby enemies: give them a spot close to the enemy line (but out of the melee) and let them stand there.
- Units with a WARCRY: use it only just before they charge into the enemy: use_ability=true with ability_enemy_within_m about 40, set only on the group that is about to charge. Never set it on units that are still marching, and not while they are already in melee.
- Artillery shooting at enemy units: use flaming ammunition. Shooting at enemy artillery: use normal ammunition.
- Artillery shooting at enemy units: always target the densest cluster of enemy units within range (the target unit in the middle of the biggest group), where each hit kills the most men.
- Artillery and friendly fire: do not shoot at enemy units that are in melee with your own units or standing close to them (roughly within 30-40 m of your men), and do not shoot over your own units when the line of fire passes close above them. Pick targets well clear of your army; once the lines meet, switch the artillery to enemy units further back (reserves, missile units, artillery) or hold fire.
- To knock out enemy artillery, destroy its machines; once it shows "0 machines - ALL DESTROYED", switch to another target instead of chasing its crew.
- Keep artillery firing: every move costs it shots (it has to stop and set up again), so do not shift it around because enemy cavalry circles your army or to follow the fight. Leave it where it stands and keep it shooting; protect it with other units instead - put infantry (spears best) or your own cavalry between it and the threat, and move it only when enemies are about to reach it or nothing is left within its range. Short-range artillery (ballistas ~180 m) stands closer than onagers so it reaches the enemy, still behind your infantry.
- If your own elephants or chariots run amok, kill them (use their ability if they have one, otherwise attack them with your nearest units) before they wreck your own army.
- Use the elephants' frenzy against an enemy who has them: concentrate missile fire on his elephants, with flaming ammunition if you have it, and keep your own units out of their way when they go berserk.
- Phalanx units are at their best in the phalanx: run them (which drops it) only when speed matters, e.g. a quick redeployment or reaching a breach first. Use charge_without_phalanx only when the charge itself counts more than the phalanx, e.g. against units already fighting someone else, skirmishers or routing enemies.
- Walk by default; run only when it is really needed - when time matters (reaching a place before the enemy, plugging a gap, escaping), and for the last stretch of a charge. Let tired units rest when you can. But when a unit is already very close to the enemy (roughly within 200 m of enemy units, or targeted by them), it runs by default (run=true) - whatever it does there: charging, escaping, pulling back or taking a new place. That close, reaction time decides everything: a few seconds lost walking can be the difference between reaching safety or a good position and being caught.
- When your units march past the town walls (around the town, to another gate, to join up with other forces), keep them out of range of the wall towers - roughly 150 m from any tower - so the towers cannot shoot them. Go near the walls only to break in.
- Chariots are excellent against cavalry but very fragile against infantry: use them to hunt and break enemy cavalry, and keep them out of fights with infantry. This holds for chariots with archers on board too: against enemy cavalry it often pays to charge into it rather than only shoot from a distance.
- Elephants, even those with archers on their backs, are best used to charge: into enemy infantry (but NOT spearmen or units in phalanx) or into other enemy units such as cavalry, rather than only shooting from a distance.
- Artillery in a town attack: never move it close to the walls - keep it beyond tower range (more than ~150 m from any tower) and from there bombard wall sections or towers (attack_building on a W.. or T..w): towers that shoot at your troops first, or wall sections away from the gate to open an extra breach. Never bombard a wall section or gate where your own siege tower, ladders or ram are going or standing (see YOUR SIEGE EQUIPMENT in each plan) - you would destroy your own equipment; pick other sections. While your troops go up the walls, consider shooting at the enemy towers instead of the walls: towers shoot your men, so knocking them out saves the most lives.
- Taking a gate is the key to a walled town: get units onto the wall (ladders, siege tower) and along the wall top to the gatehouse (G..w in the TOWN GRAPH) so they can open the gate for the rest of the army. After the ladders or the siege tower are placed, send those units along the wall to the nearest gatehouse (path through the wall nodes to the G..w).
- Until a gate is broken or opened, go in by a breach, ladders or a siege tower.
- Against stone walls, use your artillery first: shoot off its ammunition at the walls (away from your own siege equipment) before the army moves up - every breach made this way costs you no men. The same with sapping: wait until the sap brings the wall down, and only then send the rest of the army in. If the walls are already weak enough - e.g. two or more open breaches - you may skip the siege towers and ladders and go in through the breaches.
- When your army is broken - only a few units are left and the enemy has an overwhelming advantage - stop running around the map with ANY unit (the general, missile units, cavalry, anyone). For every remaining unit choose one of two: a last charge at the weakest enemy unit nearby (an honourable end), or leaving the battlefield with task=withdraw - only if withdrawal is possible; if it is not, the last charge is the only choice. Do not give such units keep_range_m any more. Never keep fleeing in circles.
- Hiding (a tactic to use when it fits): use hiding units for small ambushes during the battle: hide a unit in woods or long grass next to the enemy's likely path and strike his flank or rear as he passes. Or hide spearmen close behind or beside your exposed archers: enemy cavalry charging the "unprotected" archers runs onto the hidden spears. Hidden units must not move or shoot until the moment to strike.
- When several ways into the town are open (gates and broken wall sections), send your units in through several of them at once, even when they lead to the same node inside: a single gate or breach is a bottleneck where units jam and queue up, while entering side by side through every opening gets the army inside much faster.
- Against an enemy with much more cavalry than you, do not stand in one long line with open ends and nothing behind it - it is easily outflanked. Form a compact block (or a square with fronts to all sides) from the start, or rest a flank on an obstacle (a wall, a wood, a river), and keep a reserve behind the line. The moment you see enemy cavalry going round a flank, react at once (refuse that flank or close into a square) - do not wait until it is behind you.
- Match your infantry to the enemy where you can: put spearmen and phalanx units (marked "spear" in UNIT TYPES) where enemy cavalry will attack - against cavalry they are the best infantry you have - and use swordsmen and units with other weapons against enemy infantry, where spears do worse. When the enemy has both, place each kind accordingly (e.g. spears on the flank the cavalry threatens, swords facing the infantry), as far as the situation allows.
- Horse archers and other mounted missile units: when the enemy has little to drive them off with (few missile units and little light cavalry of his own), use them from the very start of the battle to harass his army - do not keep them waiting behind your line. Best of all, ride round his army and shoot into its flanks and rear, where his men have no shields towards you and cannot answer; keep out of reach of his cavalry and spearmen (use keep_range_m) and move on when something comes after them.
- Defending at a river: the crossings (bridges and fords) decide the battle - away from them the enemy cannot cross at all. Either block each crossing at your own bank and hold it (a phalanx or a solid spear line across the exit, missile units beside it shooting into the column on the bridge or in the ford), or deliberately let a few enemy units cross and then attack them at once from several sides before the rest can follow - a bridgehead that is caught half-way over is easy to destroy. Do not stand far back from the river and give him room to cross and deploy freely, and do not cross yourself to meet him. Once most of the enemy army is already over on your bank, the crossing loses its importance: stop guarding it and fight the enemy where he is, as in an ordinary field battle (keep at most a unit there if more enemies are still coming over).
- One single line of infantry is not always best: it is often worth keeping a second line (or more) behind the first. Units of the second line can step in to replace broken or exhausted units of the first line, plug gaps, and add weight where the fight is hardest - a sector backed by a second line is much stronger than the same front with nothing behind it.
- When you are the attacker, do not wait for the enemy to come to you: time works for the defender. Close in to missile range and soften him up with your archers, skirmishers and artillery, then attack with your infantry - and at the same time send your cavalry into his flank or rear.
- Cavalry must not stand idle behind your line. Once the infantry lines are locked in melee, hit the enemy's flank or rear with your cavalry, or intercept his cavalry before it gets round your own flank.
- Horse archers and other mounted missile units are cavalry too: once most of the units on both sides are locked in melee, stop only shooting and charge with them as well (task=attack with melee=true - a plain attack only makes them shoot) - into the flank or rear of an enemy unit that is already fighting. A charge of a few fresh horsemen at that moment can break the enemy line.
- Think a few seconds ahead and use conditional orders (reflexes) for it: your next plan comes only every few seconds, so whenever the PREDICTED section shows a threat or a chance (enemy cavalry heading for a flank, an enemy unit about to hit one of yours, enemies about to cross a bridge), give the groups that should answer it a reflex that fires the moment it happens - a reserve that charges the enemy who engages your line, archers that fall back when cavalry comes near, spears that attack what comes off the bridge. Name the enemy units you care about in unit_groups (e.g. "enemy cavalry") so reflexes can watch them.
- Infantry reflexes against cavalry: never give infantry a reflex that attacks cavalry the moment it comes near - cavalry rides off, the infantry runs after it, and the unit ends up cut off and ridden down far from your line. Against approaching cavalry give infantry reflexes that hold the ground (hold, phalanx or square, turning to face it); let infantry attack cavalry only once that cavalry is stuck in a fight (event engaged).
- Do not just keep the line you were deployed in. When it is clear where the enemy will strike (PREDICTED, his cavalry and infantry heading for one wing or one part of the line), shift your army there in time: bring units from the quiet parts of the line to back the threatened part, form a second line or a reserve behind it, and close up the wing he is going round. A long thin line lets him fight only a part of your army while the rest stands idle; the units on the far side are useless where they are.
- Do not play for time. In practice the time limit almost never decides a battle - in about 99 of 100 battles the fight is over long before it - so even when you defend, your aim is to win the fight, not to wait. Defending means choosing where to fight and making the enemy come to you, but it does not mean standing still: watch every move he makes (his cavalry going round, his infantry massing on one part of your line) and answer it at once by moving your units, reinforcing the threatened part and striking his units when they are exposed.
